## H1

How it works

## Intro

Four steps, and the car stays where it is.

## H2 — 1. We agree a value

You tell us what you drive and what it costs you to run. We assess the car and agree a value
with you. That value sets the lease, so it matters more than anything else in the arrangement.
[OWN-010, ELG-010]

## H2 — 2. We buy it

We pay you the agreed value and the car becomes ours for the term of the lease. [OWN-010]

## H2 — 3. Your employer novates the lease

The lease is between you and us. A novation agreement sits alongside it and passes the
responsibility for the lease payments to your employer while you're employed there. [EMP-010]
They deduct an agreed amount from your pay each cycle, and a portion of it comes out before
tax. [SAV-010, SAV-060] The deduction shows on your payslip and nothing else about your employment
changes.

## H2 — 4. You keep driving

Service it where you always have. Send us the invoice and we reimburse you out of your fund.
[SVC-010]

## H2 — Where the money goes

<!-- note: diagram asset at /assets/money-flow.svg — source file "13 - money flow diagram.svg"
in this folder. It replaces about 150 words of explanation. Caption below. -->

**[DIAGRAM: money flow]**

*Caption:* Three amounts go into the fund each pay cycle. Everything the car costs comes back
out of it.

The fund is what makes the arrangement useful. Money goes into it every pay cycle and comes out when a bill arrives.

When you claim, we reimburse you from what has accumulated, so what you can claim back is
limited to what's in there. [SVC-010] If you know a large bill is coming, you can build the
balance ahead of it.

---

## H2 — At the end of the term

There's a residual amount to pay. [EOT-010] You can pay it and buy the car back, re-lease it, or
hand it back. We'll set out what each one costs before you sign anything.
